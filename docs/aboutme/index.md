#About Me
Howdy y'all! My name is Ethan Cornett, and I am a Mechanical Engineering student at UNC Charlotte. My interest in engineering began in middle school when I joined VEX Robotics. What started as an interest in building robots grew into a lasting interest in understanding how things work, designing solutions, and improving existing ideas. Nearly nine years later, that experience continues to influence the way I approach engineering.

As a mechanical engineer, I am most interested in taking an idea from a concept on paper to something that can actually work in the real world. I enjoy working with CAD, analyzing mechanical systems, manufacturing components, and testing designs to see where they succeed and where they fall short. My experience with robotics taught me that a design rarely works perfectly on the first attempt—and honestly, that is part of what I enjoy about engineering. When something does not work, I want to know why. I like taking a problem apart, figuring out what went wrong, and improving the design until it performs the way it should. To me, engineering is not about getting everything right on the first try; it is about being willing to learn from what went wrong and using that information to make the next version better.

As a developing engineer, I came to UNC Charlotte from Asheville with a better understanding of how I wanted to learn engineering. My previous experience with engineering education showed me that a heavily theoretical approach was not where I found my strongest interest. I enjoy learning the theory, but I am most engaged when I can take that knowledge and apply it to something tangible. At UNC Charlotte, I have found that hands-on design, manufacturing, and problem-solving better match the way I want to develop as an engineer. I want to do more than understand why something should work on paper; I want to build it, test it, find out where it fails, and figure out how to make it better.

"What does it mean to defend an engineering decision: and do you currently know how to do it?

At the beginning of this semester, I believe an engineer should be able to defend their decisions. I think defending an engineering decision is similar to defending something you are passionate about. If someone insults your favorite sports team or movie, you are probably going to explain why you like it and why you think they are wrong. In my mind, an engineer's design is similar. A design represents the decisions, reasoning, and effort that an engineer has put into solving a problem, so I believe they should be willing to stand behind it.

I also think engineering can be an ego-heavy industry, but I do not necessarily see that as a bad thing. When people believe their ideas are the best, it can push them to work harder, challenge other people's ideas, and produce better solutions. Competition can be useful when it encourages people to improve rather than simply prove that they are right.

At the same time, I am still learning where the line is between defending a design and becoming too attached to it. I currently believe that defending a decision should mean being able to explain and support it with evidence, not refusing to admit when it is wrong. If testing, analysis, or someone else's argument proves that my design needs to change, I should be willing to change it. At Week 1, I think one of the most important things I can learn this semester is how to balance confidence in my engineering decisions with the humility to recognize when those decisions can be improved.

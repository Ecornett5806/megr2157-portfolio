Portfolio-1 MEGR 2156 Sophomore Design Morgan Gregory
https://uncc.instructure.com/eportfolios/4731/home/landing
A-Navigation:
Morgan's portfolio has a structured "landing" page that acts as his homepage, with easily accessible links on the side connecting to each assignment and an About Me page. These links remain available on each page, so the reader does not have to return to the homepage to move between assignments. This organization makes the portfolio easier to navigate and reduces the time required to locate a specific assignment, helping it meet the 60-second standard.

B-Reproducibility: 
Morgan's portfolio demonstrates strong reproducibility because the documentation provides detailed, step-by-step explanations of how he completed each assignment. The portfolio includes the information necessary for another student or colleague to understand the process and recreate the work without needing to ask Morgan for additional information.

C- Evidence of reasoning:
Morgan shows extensive reasoning and the processes he used to arrive at his final answer. For example, in Assignment 2, he explains why he uses the largest member force to calculate his cross-sectional area and why he chose a square cross-section for the truss. He states, "I want to make my beams square shaped for simplicity." Although the wording is informal, the statement provides reasoning for his design choice rather than simply presenting the final result. This shows that Morgan documents the reasoning behind his design decisions and allows the reader to understand how he arrived at his solution.

D- Professional tone: 
Morgan's portfolio is somewhat professional, but at some points it strays a little bit from a professional tone. I say this because he explains his thought process in a way engineering students and professors alike could understand but his wording is what makes his tone shift to slightly informal based on the words he uses such as "I want to make my beams square shaped for simplicity" it could be worded a little more technically in a professional setting, such as a brief explanation to why the square cross section simplifies the design.
